# F2: Spatial-unit choice in 30 m crop monitoring

## Findings

### [1] Parcel-level aggregation of pixel time series has been reported to raise crop-classification overall accuracy from 0.648 to 0.721 (Xu, as cited in Gou et al. 2026), which is the direct precedent for field-aggregate representations in small fields.
- quote: "Xu9, compared pixel-based classification with structured parcel-level strategies and found that parcel-based method improved overall accuracy from 0.648 to 0.721."
- url: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-68078-5
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-08-21
- confidence: high

### [2] In fragmented smallholdings, fields can contain fewer than ten 10 m Sentinel-2 pixels, and diagonal field boundaries against the pixel grid create systematic edge mixing that makes pixel-wise labels unreliable — the same argument applies with greater force at 30 m Landsat resolution.
- quote: "Such parcel sizes correspond to fewer than roughly ten Sentinel-2 10 m pixels per field, motivating the parcel-level aggregation adopted in this study to mitigate boundary and mixed-pixel effects."
- url: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-68078-5
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-08-21
- confidence: high

### [3] The same study states explicitly that diagonal parcel boundaries induce stair-step aliasing and systematic edge mixing, so the authors clipped images to parcels and averaged all in-boundary pixels to suppress mixed-pixel noise.
- quote: "This issue is exacerbated when parcel boundaries run diagonally to the pixel grid, inducing stair-step aliasing that creates systematic edge mixing. These effects make pixel-wise labels unreliable for accurate classification."
- url: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-68078-5
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-08-21
- confidence: high

### [4] STS-NET (Dalal et al. 2026), the closest published analogue to a label-scarce sugarcane stress task, operates on single-pixel time-series vectors (T=32 timesteps, C=5 channels: RGB, NIR, mean of four VIs) rather than spatial patches, and validates on a 2.5-acre, 62-field sugarcane experiment.
- quote: "The final dataset was organized as spatio-temporal pixel-level time series vectors for model training."
- url: https://arxiv.org/html/2607.18791v1
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-07-21
- confidence: high

### [5] STS-NET reports 97.98% water-stress and 83.47% combined water–nitrogen accuracy with a 19.2K-parameter 3D-CAE encoder, versus 400K parameters for the pixel-time-series SSL baseline Presto, and explicitly attributes its gains to resolution and denser temporal sampling; note it has no random-init encoder control and is evaluated within one experimental farm.
- quote: "Remarkably, our architecture operates with only 19.2K parameters substantially lower than Presto's 400K [22] highlighting its efficiency in both accuracy and computational cost."
- url: https://arxiv.org/html/2607.18791v1
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-07-21
- confidence: high

### [6] Presto (Tseng et al. 2024) is a pixel-time-series SSL model, and for image-level tasks it aggregates all pixel embeddings by concatenated per-dimension mean and standard deviation — a field-aggregate readout directly comparable to averaging/second-moment pooling over Landsat pixels inside a field polygon.
- quote: "we calculated the mean and standard deviation of these N output tokens per dimension and concatenated the result, yielding a 2 de-dimensional vector"
- url: https://arxiv.org/html/2304.14065v4
- source_type: primary
- published: 2024-02-05
- confidence: high

### [7] In the S2-Agri100 parcel benchmark, each parcel is represented by a 5×5 pixel patch (24 timesteps, 100 parcels per crop for train/validation); pretrained Presto reached 68.89% OA versus 67.03% for pretrained SITS-Former and 45.98% for randomly initialized Presto — a published example of a small spatial patch and of a legitimate pretraining gain against a random-init control.
- quote: "a 5×5 pixel patch from each parcel is used for input"
- url: https://arxiv.org/html/2304.14065v4
- source_type: primary
- published: 2024-02-05
- confidence: high

### [8] Presto's own ablation quantifies the SSL advantage on that small patch benchmark as roughly a 50% relative accuracy increase over random initialization, and the authors state the effect is task-dependent (on TreeSatAI S1, random init slightly beat pretraining).
- quote: "Pre-training yielded a significant increase in performance (a 50% increase in accuracy on the S2-Agri100 dataset)."
- url: https://arxiv.org/html/2304.14065v4
- source_type: primary
- published: 2024-02-05
- confidence: high

### [9] A 2025/2026 Sentinel-2 LULC patch-size study explicitly compared 5×5, 7×7 and 64×64 patches and found the smallest patch best (97.6% accuracy, kappa 0.95, F1 97%), supporting small neighbourhoods rather than large contextual windows at medium resolution — though full text is paywalled, so this is an abstract-level claim.
- quote: "The results indicate that the patch size 5 × 5 outperforms an accuracy of 97.6%, a kappa coefficient of 0.95, and an F1 score of 97% compared to patches 7 × 7 and 64 × 64."
- url: https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-032-21013-5_16
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-05-01
- confidence: medium

### [10] Vincent et al. (2024) show the spatial-context payoff directly: the image-level UTAE model (which sees neighbourhood context) beat the best pixel-sequence methods on PASTIS by +2.0 points OA and +5.5 points mean accuracy, with folds separated by at least 1 km to prevent spatial leakage.
- quote: "As expected, since UTAE can leverage knowledge on the spatial context of each pixel, it achieves the best score on PASTIS dataset by +2.0% in OA and +5.5% in MA."
- url: https://arxiv.org/html/2303.12533v2
- source_type: primary
- published: 2024-07-12
- confidence: high

### [11] The same benchmark finds the ordering reverses under year-to-year domain shift (DENETHOR, train 2018 / test 2019): simple, deformation-invariant nearest-centroid methods on pixel sequences beat all complex MTSC models — a warning that any patch-size advantage seen within one season may not transfer across years.
- quote: "Because of their complexity, other methods struggle to deal with this domain shift. In this setting, our extension of NCC to incorporate specific meaningful deformations achieves better performances than all the other MTSC methods we evaluated."
- url: https://arxiv.org/html/2303.12533v2
- source_type: primary
- published: 2024-07-12
- confidence: high

### [12] A smallholder sugarcane/rice/wheat study (Khan et al., Sensors 2023; 2600 mapped fields, mean 1.2 acres) averaged per-pixel band and index values over each field into a 30×15 LSTM input, and explicitly criticises patch-based studies for ignoring field boundaries and pulling in neighbouring crops.
- quote: "Additionally, actual field boundaries are not considered in mapping crops as they may have irregular shapes, thus incorporating neighboring fields with different crop types."
- url: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9967001/
- source_type: primary
- published: 2023-02-05
- confidence: high

## Dead ends
- `websearch` tool returned HTTP 402 on all three initial queries; searches were run by fetching DuckDuckGo HTML pages instead.
- DuckDuckGo HTML started returning a bot CAPTCHA on the fourth query, so no further search refinement was possible.
- MDPI (`https://www.mdpi.com/2673-2688/5/2/33`) and ScienceDirect (`S0034425720305290`, 30 m NDVI reconstruction; `S2773186326000320`, mixed-pixel crop mapping) returned HTTP 403; their snippets were seen but not cited because the pages were not fetched.
- No study was found that directly compares single-pixel vs 3×3–11×11 Landsat patches vs field aggregates for *anomaly/stress detection* (most spatial-unit evidence comes from crop-type classification or stress classification with PlanetScope/Very-high-resolution data).
- "Scale-space-based patch selection for crop classification in satellite images" appeared in search results but was not fetched (ProQuest/Bohrium aggregators), so no claim from it is made.

## Suggested follow-ups
- Find a peer-reviewed study that reports an optimal patch size specifically for Landsat 30 m crop *stress/anomaly* tasks (as opposed to crop-type mapping), to see whether 3×3 or 5×5 is justified at 30 m.
- Check whether any SSL crop-stress paper reports a random-init encoder control alongside field-aggregate vs pixel-unit inputs on the same one/few fields (STS-NET and Presto each provide only part of this comparison).
- Examine how mixed-pixel fraction is quantified near field boundaries (e.g. pure-pixel erosion or sub-pixel unmixing) and whether it is used to weight or mask training samples in 30 m SITS models.
