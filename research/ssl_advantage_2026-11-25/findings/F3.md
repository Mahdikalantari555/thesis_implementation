# F3: Controls required for a defensible SSL advantage claim

## Findings

### [1] The canonical RS SSL benchmark makes the advantage claim only against a randomly initialized encoder of the same ResNet-18 backbone plus a fully supervised upper bound, not against arbitrary smaller models.
- quote: "In addition, we report the performance of fully supervised learning and training with randomly initialized encoders for more thorough comparison."
- url: https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2206.13188
- source_type: primary
- published: 2022-06-27 (v1; revised 2022-09-02; accepted IEEE Geoscience and Remote Sensing Magazine)
- confidence: high

### [2] Collapse must be detected during pretraining, not inferred from downstream numbers: the same RS benchmark found SimSiam's loss dropped in the first 10 epochs then became unstable, forcing them to stop at 10 epochs and to add color jitter only for that method.
- quote: "The loss goes down quickly in the beginning 10 epochs and becomes very unstable in the following epochs."
- url: https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2206.13188
- source_type: primary
- published: 2022-06-27 (v1; revised 2022-09-02)
- confidence: high

### [3] The SSL advantage in the RS benchmark is a label-scarcity effect: linear probing beat supervised learning only at 1% labels, while fine-tuning beat it across label fractions; the headline "50% SSL labels beats 100% supervised" is a separate fine-tuning result.
- quote: "linear classification with self-supervision outperforms vanilla supervised learning when reducing the amount of available labels to 1%"
- url: https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2206.13188
- source_type: primary
- published: 2022-06-27 (v1; revised 2022-09-02)
- confidence: high

### [4] Randomly initialized training can match pretrained models at equal task accuracy if given more iterations — pretraining buys convergence speed, not necessarily better final accuracy or regularization — so an SSL win at unequal epoch/compute budgets is not a representation-quality result.
- quote: "Experiments show that ImageNet pre-training speeds up convergence early in training, but does not necessarily provide regularization or improve final target task accuracy."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.08883
- source_type: primary
- published: 2018-11-21
- confidence: high

### [5] VICReg supplies an explicit, inspectable collapse diagnostic — per-dimension embedding variance plus covariance decorrelation — rather than relying on architectural implicit biases; the same paper reports that adding this variance term stabilizes other SSL methods.
- quote: "a method that explicitly avoids the collapse problem with a simple regularization term on the variance of the embeddings along each dimension individually"
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.04906
- source_type: primary
- published: 2021-05-11 (v1; revised 2022-01-28; ICLR 2022)
- confidence: high

### [6] Barlow Twins turns collapse avoidance into a measurable quantity — the cross-correlation matrix of two augmented views' embeddings should approach the identity — which is a concrete diagnostic to log alongside loss in a small-data sweep.
- quote: "We propose an objective function that naturally avoids collapse by measuring the cross-correlation matrix between the outputs of two identical networks fed with distorted versions of a sample, and making it as close to the identity matrix as possible."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.03230
- source_type: primary
- published: 2021-03-04 (v1; revised 2021-06-14; ICML 2021)
- confidence: high

### [7] Backbone design is a confound in SSL comparisons: architecture choices can dominate pretext-task choices, so an "architecture/patch-size effect" must be isolated with the same pretraining objective and the same parameter/compute budget.
- quote: "We challenge a number of common practices in selfsupervised visual representation learning and observe that standard recipes for CNN design do not always translate to self-supervised representation learning."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.09005
- source_type: primary
- published: 2019-01-25
- confidence: high

### [8] A concrete small-sample negative result: under leakage-safe Leave-One-Region-Out evaluation over 14 regions / 31 images, a hand-crafted LBP+SVM (balanced accuracy 0.79) beat SSL convolutional embeddings pretrained on 143 unlabeled images and all deep models — so a hand-crafted/rule baseline is mandatory before claiming SSL helps at small n.
- quote: "The simplest texture approach, LBP with a support vector machine (LBP+SVM), performed best, achieving a balanced accuracy of 0.79, H2 recall of 0.69, and H2 precision of 0.82, outperforming every deep-learning and combined-feature model."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28567
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-09-23 (preprint; domain = SEM micrographs, not EO)
- confidence: medium

### [9] The matching statistical control is a group-level label-permutation null at the unit of independence (region/field), not a pixel- or sample-level p-value; in the same small-n study 500 permutations from 3,003 possible region-to-label assignments gave p = 0.008.
- quote: "A group-level permutation test (500 permutations sampled from the 3,003 possible region-to-label assignments) yielded p = 0.008, indicating that the result cannot be explained by a chance alignment of the region structure."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28567
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-09-23 (preprint; domain = SEM micrographs, not EO)
- confidence: medium

### [10] A published EO anomaly-detection SSL result is made defensible by (a) classical and simple sequential baselines, (b) an independent pseudo-ground-truth modality never used by the model, and (c) a permutation test — on 300 time series over three AOIs: ROC-AUC 77.0% vs ~50% for RX, PaDiM, Linear AR and LSTM, with 99.9% spatial coherence at p < 0.001.
- quote: "On the DFC 2026 dataset (300 time-series, three AOIs), T-SAR-JEPA achieves ROC-AUC of 77.0% on the Hawaii eruption window, outperforming RX, PaDiM, Linear AR, and LSTM baselines (~50%)."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05700
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-06-04 (IGARSS 2026; IEEE GRSS Data Fusion Contest 2026 winner)
- confidence: medium

### [11] A direct precedent for judging a small-sample architecture comparison: a Mamba-vs-Transformer benchmark with a shared pipeline reported a 1.3-percentage-point gap but explicitly refused a definitive ranking because it lacked per-seed variance and significance tests, and called for capacity/compute controls.
- quote: "These results are preliminary because they do not include variance estimates across random seeds or statistical significance testing."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.00141
- source_type: primary
- published: 2025-08-29 (v1; revised 2026-10-07; preprint)
- confidence: medium

### [12] Pre-registration is normal practice for method comparisons in neighbouring statistical literature — one preregistered simulation study compared 28 methods over 11 datasets — but no preregistered SSL-vs-random-init study in EO was found.
- quote: "We therefore conducted a preregistered, simulation-based comparison of 28 established methods for variable selection and inference under model uncertainty, using 11 empirical datasets spanning a range of sample sizes and number of predictors"
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.23216
- source_type: primary
- published: 2025-11-28 (v1; revised 2026-05-01; PNAS DOI 10.1073/pnas.2534552123)
- confidence: medium

## Suggested follow-ups
- Does any peer-reviewed EO/time-series paper report per-seed spreads for SSL vs a same-architecture random-init control at a fixed epoch/compute budget, and what is the smallest n at which the gap survives?
- What is the published crossover point (number of fields/time series) at which a classical baseline (RX, isolation forest, SVM/random forest on NDVI statistics) stops matching a time-series SSL detector?
- Are there EO anomaly-detection papers using a field-level (group) label-permutation null instead of pixel-level p-values, and what permutation count do they use?
