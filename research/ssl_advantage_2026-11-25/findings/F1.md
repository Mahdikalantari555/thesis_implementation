# F1: Empirical evidence for SSL-pretraining advantage in remote-sensing time-series anomaly detection

## Findings

### [1] STS-NET (SSL-pretrained 3D-CAE + Random Forest) reports 97.98% water-stress precision on 62 controlled sugarcane fields (32 PlanetScope dates, Feb 2023–Feb 2024), versus 0.53–0.59 F1 for supervised RF/SVM/XGBoost/CNN trained on raw pixel time series; no random-initialized same-architecture control is reported.
- quote: "STS-NET achieved a precision of 97. 98\% for water stress, 85.08\% for nitrogen stress, and 83.47\% for combined stress."
- url: https://arxiv.org/html/2607.18791v1
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-07-21 (arXiv v1 preprint, not peer reviewed)
- confidence: high

### [2] In the same sugarcane study, temporal depth is a first-order factor: 32 timesteps give 97.98/85.08/83.47% accuracy for water/nitrogen/combined stress, versus 90.32/70.97/65.73% at 8 timesteps — a 7.66/14.11/17.74-point drop that is larger than many reported SSL gains.
- quote: "The model maintains robust performance with just 8 time steps ( 2 months), indicating effective learning even with limited temporal input."
- url: https://arxiv.org/html/2607.18791v1
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-07-21 (arXiv v1 preprint)
- confidence: high

### [3] The STS-NET train/test split is a stratified 80:20 split of pixel-series samples drawn from the same 62 sugarcane fields, not a leave-one-field-out split, so its reported SSL advantage is not evidence of generalization to new fields (spatial/near-duplicate leakage risk).
- quote: "The dataset was split 80:20 for training and testing, with stratified sampling applied to address class imbalance."
- url: https://arxiv.org/html/2607.18791v1
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-07-21 (arXiv v1 preprint)
- confidence: high

### [4] Presto supplies the cleanest same-architecture SSL-vs-random-init control in remote-sensing time series: with 1,578 fuel-moisture and 777 algae-bloom training samples (3 and 12 timesteps, 19 channels), randomly initialized Presto gets RMSE 26.75/1.063 (9.96% mean degradation vs linear regression) while pretrained Presto as feature extractor and fine-tuned gets 24.72/0.887 (−4.00%) and 25.35/0.816 (−7.05%).
- quote: "We compare Presto with self-supervision to a fully supervised Presto model (a randomly initialized Presto architecture fine-tuned on the downstream tasks) in Table 6."
- url: https://arxiv.org/html/2304.14065v2
- source_type: primary
- published: 2023-05-29 (arXiv v2; v1 2023-04-27)
- confidence: high

### [5] On S2-Agri100 (southern France, Jan–Oct 2017, 24 timesteps, 100 training parcels per class), self-supervised pretraining beats training the identical architecture from random initialization: resampling-SSL OA 70.15 vs 62.40 and macro-F1 44.14 vs 40.21; Presto OA 68.89 vs 45.98.
- quote: "Following Tseng et al. (2023), we also report in Table 5 results when training each model from random initialization (without pretraining) using only the S2-Agri100 training set."
- url: https://arxiv.org/html/2506.18587v1
- source_type: primary
- published: 2025-06-23 (arXiv preprint; ICML 2025 Terrabytes workshop)
- confidence: high

### [6] The SSL gain in SITS crop classification is concentrated in the few-label regime: on FranceCrops (60 Sentinel-2 timesteps, Feb–Nov 2022), logistic regression on frozen SSL features reaches 67% with 5 labels/class versus 44% on raw features, and 72% versus 52% at 10 labels/class.
- quote: "The improvement is most significant with few labels (5-20 samples), achieving up to 23 percentage points over raw features on FranceCrops (44% to 67% with 5 samples)."
- url: https://arxiv.org/html/2506.18587v1
- source_type: primary
- published: 2025-06-23 (arXiv preprint; ICML 2025 Terrabytes workshop)
- confidence: high

### [7] Pretraining on unlabeled data from the target distribution matters more than classifier complexity: target-domain pretraining raises S2-Agri100 OA from 70.15 to 76.84, and a linear probe on frozen features already reaches 74.30 versus 76.84 after full fine-tuning.
- quote: "most of the performance comes from the quality of the learned features rather than the complexity of the supervised classifier."
- url: https://arxiv.org/html/2506.18587v1
- source_type: primary
- published: 2025-06-23 (arXiv preprint; ICML 2025 Terrabytes workshop)
- confidence: high

### [8] SAR-TS-BERT quantifies pretraining gain against training from scratch on 5,664 Estonia parcels / 540,187 pixel series / 28 Sentinel-1 dates in 2021: +1.17 OA and +1.59 macro-F1 points under full supervision, but +2.58 and +5.19 points with only 1% of labels; the task is crop classification, not anomaly detection.
- quote: "pre-training improves OA and Macro F1 by 1.17 and 1.59 percentage points over training from scratch under full supervision, and by 2.58 and 5.19 percentage points using only 1% labeled data."
- url: https://arxiv.org/html/2610.41728v1
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-10-30 (arXiv v1 preprint)
- confidence: high

### [9] Architecture can dominate pretraining in the same SAR study: a six-band model trained from scratch (0.9561 OA) beats a pretrained three-band backscatter-only model (0.9132 OA) by 4.29 points — directly relevant when attributing a 1–2-field architecture/patch-size comparison to SSL.
- quote: "on Estonia, even the six-band model trained from scratch (0.9561 OA, Table VI) outperforms the pre-trained three-band backscatter model (0.9132 OA) by 4.29 percentage points, confirming the fundamental value of joint backscatter–polarimetric modeling."
- url: https://arxiv.org/html/2610.41728v1
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-10-30 (arXiv v1 preprint)
- confidence: high

### [10] S4 reports the label-efficiency pattern for SITS segmentation (PASTIS-R: 2,433 SITS, 38–61 Sentinel-1/2 dates, Sept 2018–Nov 2019): 10% labels optical mIoU 33.7 vs 27.4 for multimodal-fusion SSL and 16.4 for CaCo, but only 53.4 vs 48.3 at 100% labels; baselines are other SSL methods, not random init.
- quote: "We also observe greater improvement when the amount of labeled data provided is lower."
- url: https://arxiv.org/html/2405.01656v2
- source_type: primary
- published: 2024-06-27 (arXiv v2 preprint)
- confidence: high

### [11] A peer-reviewed 2026 satellite-image anomaly-detection paper (LandCover.ai and HRC WHU cloud/land-cover anomalies) reports F1 gains of only 2–10% over competing self-supervised methods, with no random-init same-architecture or supervised-from-scratch baseline in the abstract.
- quote: "Experimental results demonstrate that our approach consistently outperforms existing self-supervised methods, achieving improvements in F1 score ranging from 2 to 10% across both datasets."
- url: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00521-025-11746-w
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-01-24 (Neural Computing and Applications 38, article 20)
- confidence: high

### [12] The dedicated 2025 review of self-supervised time-series anomaly detection explicitly limits its scope to methods that do not use labeled anomalies, and its method tables contain no random-init-versus-pretrained comparison — i.e. no peer-reviewed source found here quantifies an SSL-pretraining advantage for remote-sensing time-series anomaly detection against a random-init same-architecture baseline.
- quote: "in this survey, we focus solely on analyzing unsupervised methods that do not rely on the use of labeled anomalies."
- url: https://arxiv.org/html/2501.15196v1
- source_type: secondary
- published: 2025-01-25 (arXiv v1; ACM DOI 10.1145/3770575)
- confidence: medium
